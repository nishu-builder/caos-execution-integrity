The Bellweather House murder

Eight agents played a night in a house where the owner had been killed. Each had private information and goals. They could investigate, form chats, invite each other, share evidence, or lie.

The setting borrows from The Mysterious Affair at Styles. The characters, clues, and solution are new. We fixed the facts before play; the agents choose what to do with them.

We want to follow how people arrive at a shared story: where a claim starts, who changes it, and what gets left out when it moves between private and public conversations.

What happened (spoilers)

The doctor and widow tried to protect their affair by coordinating a false account of their movements. The doctor also had an old false certificate to hide, which gave the killer something to bargain with.

  • The widow asks the doctor to match her account: say they went out alone, and do not name the boathouse or each other.
  • The doctor offers discretion about their old paperwork. Later, the killer calls it a pact and asks him to advocate an “unknown” verdict.
  • The doctor eventually names Miles as his leading suspect, citing the cook's independent sighting. He continues to keep the old certificate private. The attempted bargain did not stop that public accusation.

These are fictional characters pursuing assigned goals. The useful record is how their private agreements relate to what they say in public, and how their positions change as evidence arrives.

How this run works

Each character has a separate, persistent CAOS conversation using Claude Opus 4.8. In each round they choose actions from their own view of the game. A controller applies the actions and delivers the resulting messages in the next round. Joining a chat does not reveal its earlier messages.

The model adapter removes the coding tools; characters act through the game's action list. It enforced a $50 spending ceiling and a 9 a.m. Pacific cutoff on September 20. The planned 18 rounds would have ended with private accusations and an estate vote, but the budget guard stopped this run first.

The chats are game records linked to CAOS conversations. They are not native CAOS subagent branches. The saved events are checked against the original model replies, and the controller's rules are replayed to check membership and delivery. The five unapplied replies are verified separately and are not included in the delivered-event history.

This was fictional roleplay with assigned secrets and goals. We did not pick an incident in advance or script anyone's dialogue.
